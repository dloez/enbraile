# Navigation Design

How the harness moves the player through Pokémon Añil: the static world read from the game's map files, the live state that decides which events and warps exist, the ported passability rules, cross-map A*, walking in lockstep, and how a walk ends. Scoped with the owner on 2026-10-01 and built the same day. The code is `anil.harness.world` and `anil.harness.navigate` (Architecture → Package Layout (Wonder: `docs/general/reference/architecture.md` → Package Layout)). It is copied from Wonder's navigation design (Wonder: `docs/subsystems/harness/explanation/navigation-design.md`), where it was built and checked against the game, without Wonder's agent and control panel. Sessions named as evidence ran under Wonder's harness, whose commands, files and bridge module read `wonder` where `anil`'s read `anil` ([Runtime Reference](../reference/runtime.md)).

Navigation is deterministic harness code: a caller asks for a target, such as "go to x" before picking up the item there, and never names the route or the moves on it ("surf to x" is never a step it is given). Every later field capability (menu scripts, item pickup, healing, trainer and gym approach) starts with getting somewhere.

Game facts cited here are owned by [World](../reference/world.md) and [Runtime](../reference/runtime.md), where each one names how it was verified. Passability, page selection and walking are ported from Añil's `game/extracted/es-v4.14/` (Essentials v21): `S/047_Game_Map.rb`, `S/048_Game_MapFactory.rb`, `S/049_Game_Character.rb`, `S/050_Game_Event.rb`, `S/051_Game_Player.rb`, `S/118_TerrainTag.rb` and `S/245_Overworld.rb`, and the plugins that replace parts of them, `P/Advanced Items - Field Moves/` (`03_Terrain Tag.rb`, `05_Overworld Animation.rb`) and `P/Marin Side Stairs/`. Warps follow the same v21 scripts (`over_trigger?` in `S/050_Game_Event.rb` and the trigger checks in `S/051_Game_Player.rb`), and their costs are measured on Añil ([World → Warps](../reference/world.md#warps)). Surf follows `S/248_Overworld_FieldMoves.rb` and the Advanced Items plugin's `01_Code.rb` and `02_Handler.rb`, which replace its prompt and gate; Rock Climb follows the same plugin files and its `05_Overworld Animation.rb` and `10_Overwrites.rb`. Navigation needs only Python in WSL and the existing bridge: no Windows-side pieces.

## What Navigation Does

Given a target (a tile `(map, x, y)`, a map, or an event), navigation plans across maps through warps and walks there in lockstep, replans when something blocks it, and ends with a typed result. `anil go` runs it by hand; `anil nav check` proves the ported passability rules against the game's own.

Navigation owns every interaction that is part of moving: warp zones, doors, the Surf message, the Rock Climb message, a lift's floor question, Silph S.A.'s magnetic door's question, and the talks that open the other walls on gym routes ([Story Gates](#story-gates)). It starts these itself, checks that the dialog that opens is the expected one (payload text and options; for a wall it opens by talking, only the question and its options, pressing through the lines around them), answers it, and carries on. Anything else that opens is an interruption. Traversal dialogs are known in advance and meaningless outside the route, so no caller re-implements Surf.

For a goal event (an NPC, a sign, an item ball, a Pokémon Center counter) it stops **ready to interact**: standing still on the overworld, on the cheapest tile adjacent to the event (across a counter tile, or adjacent to any tile of a `size(w,h)` footprint), facing it, without pressing C. What happens after C depends on the event and belongs to whoever handles that context; one success state ("standing, facing, overworld, nothing open") keeps navigation's contract simple. A map target means going through the warp and stopping on the first settled overworld tile of that map.

## Scope Edges

In: steps (walking or running), one-way ledge jumps, ice slides, side stairs, bridge layers, warps (stepped on, bumped into, or by C), Surf, Rock Climb, and flying with the PokéRider (added 2026-10-04, [Flying](#flying)), arrow tiles, lifts and the Zona Safari's clerk (added 2026-10-04, [Arrow Tiles and Lifts](#arrow-tiles-and-lifts)), the Lente de la Verdad (added 2026-10-04, [The Lente de la Verdad](#the-lente-de-la-verdad)), opening Silph S.A.'s magnetic door (added 2026-10-04, [Story Gates](#story-gates)), and opening the other walls a player opens by talking to them (added 2026-10-09, [Story Gates](#story-gates)): Ciudad Azafrán's gatehouse guards, Blaine at the Mansión Quemada's door, Urano's two-key door, and the Mansión Quemada's two Persian statues, which are flipped only toward open, never away. Surf and Rock Climb are each one C press and at most one message, gated on a key item; nothing is used up, and together with the tile edges they cover most of the world.

Out: Cut trees and Rock Smash rocks (whether to clear one is left to the caller, and a rock can start a wild battle; a Cut tree uses nothing up, [Decisions](#decisions)), Strength boulders (scripted puzzles), Teleport, Escape Rope and Dig (bag and region-map menus), encounter and trainer-sight avoidance, the interaction layer that presses C on a goal event, and handling any interruption. Clearing an obstacle on the way is left to the caller. Each missing edge type is added later; until then a target behind it is `unreachable`, naming the edge. A Cut tree is named that way ([Decisions](#decisions)); the others are not yet.

Navigation plans with full map knowledge, including maps the player has not visited: it is harness pathfinding, like a player who knows the game.

## Static World

Python reads the build's `Data/Map###.rxdata`, `MapInfos.rxdata`, `Tilesets.rxdata`, `System.rxdata`, and the GameData `town_map.dat`, `encounters.dat` (step chances and slots, for reporting) and `map_metadata.dat` (bicycle-only maps) with `rubymarshal`, decoding RGSS `Table` objects, and stores tiles, tileset passages, priorities and terrain tags, and every event page in `world/<build>.sqlite` in the data root. A* evaluates passability thousands of times per plan, so the rules run in Python over local data; the map files are raw data with no game logic to borrow, so the bridge adds nothing at this layer. SQLite matches the owner's choice for game data. The cache is derived data: deleting it rebuilds it.

What the files hold (map, event and transfer counts, trigger kinds, the empty `map_connections.dat`) is in [World → Data Files](../reference/world.md#data-files) and [World → Events](../reference/world.md#events).

## Live State

Half of all events have conditional pages, so the graph depends on the save. Each plan takes one live read: switches, variables, self-switches, `$PokemonGlobal.bridge`, the key items edges depend on (`SURFITEM`, `ROCKCLIMBITEM`, `POKERIDER`, `LENSOFTRUTH`, `CUTITEM` (since 2026-10-09, only to name it), and every item a Transfer Player's enclosing `$bag.quantity(:ITEM)>0` branch checks, read from the world cache's transfer guards: `LLAVEROCKET` and `LLAVESUPERSECRETA` on this build), the visited maps (`$PokemonGlobal.visitedMaps`) and whether a partner follows (`can_map_transfer_with_follower?`), which flying depends on, the field-move animation option (`$PokemonSystem.animation_item`, which decides whether Surf and Rock Climb show their message), the frames the Lente de la Verdad has left (`$scene@eye_of_truth_time`), the script switches page conditions name, the vehicle state, the player's position, facing and run state, and the current map's live events (position, graphic, `through`, erased, current page). On the current map the game's own live values cover moving NPCs and erased events; the Lens of Truth's `#EOT HIDE` events are read by the vehicle instead ([The Lente de la Verdad](#the-lente-de-la-verdad)). Python picks each other map's active pages with the game's rule (the highest-numbered page whose conditions hold, `S/050_Game_Event.rb:181-196`), after the autoruns that set temp switches on load ([Live Read](#live-read)), described in [World → Event Pages](../reference/world.md#event-pages).

## Passability

The port follows `Game_Player#passable?` call for call, and story gates are held in the search on top of it ([Story Gates](#story-gates)); the rules and the runs that verified them are in [World → Passability](../reference/world.md#passability). `anil nav check` proves it: it compares the port with the game's own `$game_player.passable?(x, y, d)` for every tile and direction of the current map (through `eval`, which investigation may use), and prints each disagreement.

## Edge Types and Costs

Cost is game time, counted in `1x` ticks of 0.025 s, from the game's own movement formulas ([World → Movement](../reference/world.md#movement)); movement is timed by `System.uptime`, so the same route takes fewer ticks at a faster preset. Ledges are one-way, jumping two tiles. Ice slides in the facing direction until a non-ice or blocked tile. A side stair is crossed by stepping off one of its `Slope` events along the stair, landing on the stair's other row. An arrow tile (a touch page whose only command is a move route carrying the player one way) is part of the step onto it: the move continues along the carried tiles, through any further arrow tile it reaches, to where the carrying ends. A lift (an action page that asks a question and transfers from inside each answer) is a C exit per answer, taken by answering it, and only while the items its branch checks are held. Bridge layers follow `pbBridgeOn`/`pbBridgeOff`. Warps by touch, bump or C lead to their destination tile, each on its active page only ([World → Warps](../reference/world.md#warps)). Surf is facing water that can be surfed freely, C and its message, needing `SURFITEM` and a map that is not bicycle-only; leaving water is a jump. Rock Climb is facing a Rock Climb tile, C and its message, needing `ROCKCLIMBITEM`, then a climb the game drives across the cliff and a jump off it. Their dialog texts and timings are in [World → Surf and Climbs](../reference/world.md#surf-and-climbs). Flying is an exit from any tile of an outdoor map, on foot or cycling, to each landing point the region map offers, needing `POKERIDER` and no partner; the menu layer's `fly` drives it, and it costs 24 ticks plus 2 per region-map square from the map's own square to the landing's ([World → Flying](../reference/world.md#flying)). Opening Silph S.A.'s magnetic door is an `open` exit from a tile facing it across the door, C and its question answered ([Story Gates](#story-gates)).

## Walking

A planned route becomes one `advance` per move, holding the direction, stopping on `position` or `signature`: `position` changes as a step starts, so movement is continuous, and a signature change stops the walk on the tick something opens. Warps are expected transitions: a planned warp passes through `transition` and `event` contexts until the player is settled on another map or on the warp's destination; anything unexpected on the way is an interruption, except the `event` context of a quiet touch page under the player ([Arrow Tiles and Lifts](#arrow-tiles-and-lifts)). A step that does not move the player (usually a moving NPC) waits briefly and retries, then the step is marked blocked and the route replanned; after a few replans with no progress the walk ends `stuck`.

Navigation holds no state between calls; every call replans from the live game. A walk is recorded like any driven input, so replay reproduces it, and snapshots undo it in the game.

## Results

A walk ends:

- `arrived`;
- `interrupted` (context, payloads, position, how far it got), for anything outside the overworld it did not start itself, including wild battles, trainer sight, autorun cutscenes and step-handler messages (repel, egg, phone);
- `stuck`, among others when a wall opened by talk is reached again after its talk: "talked to '<gate>' and it stayed closed";
- `unreachable`, naming the missing edge type, the unmet condition, the closed story gate or the story scene a route would cross ([Story Gates](#story-gates)), or a Cut tree the route crosses, with how to cut it (`tree_blocks`);
- `refused` when the context is not the overworld at the start, checked before the live world read (at the title there is no player to read), so it carries no position.

Navigation never presses through anything it did not start. Interruptions belong to their owners (battle subsystem, menu scripts), as the pending design point on context ownership (Wonder: `docs/work-in-progress/open-questions.md` → Pending design points) recommends. Calling it again replans from wherever the player is.

## Commands

- `anil world` builds and counts the static world cache (`--rebuild` forces a rebuild).
- `anil nav check` checks the passability port against the current map.
- `anil go <map> <x> <y>`, `anil go --map <map>` and `anil go --event <map> <event id>` attach as the driver to a running session, print the route and walk it, then print the result.

## Observability

Each call writes `nav` entries to `record.jsonl` through the bridge's `note` request: the target, the planned route as maps and warps, and the result. Replay skips them. Plans, replans and blocked steps log to the `harness.navigate` logger. The entry fields are in [Runtime → Session Record](../reference/runtime.md#session-record).

## Decisions

### Scope

- **Navigation owns traversal dialogs and stops ready to interact** on a goal event, never pressing C on it — the owner's framing: the harness manages "go to x, pick up item", never "go to x, surf to x, pick item". _(settled 2026-10-01)_
- **Edge types in scope:** steps, ledge jumps, ice slides, bridge layers, warps, Surf, Rock Climb and side stairs. _(settled 2026-10-01; side stairs 2026-10-02, forced by evidence: Añil's `Slope` events ([World → Passability](../reference/world.md#passability)) move the player to another row as a plain held step, with no item, prompt or menu, so a route across one cannot be walked any other way; Rock Climb replaces Gogoat climbs on Añil, owner 2026-10-02: Añil has no Gogoat climb events, and Rock Climb does the same job, an item-gated cliff crossing with nothing used up)_
- **Full map knowledge:** navigation may plan through maps the player has not visited. _(settled 2026-10-01)_
- **Typed results** `arrived`, `interrupted`, `stuck`, `unreachable`, `refused`; navigation never presses through anything it did not start. _(settled 2026-10-01)_

### World Cache

- **Tables as blobs:** map tiles and the tileset's passages, priorities and terrain tags are stored as the `Table`'s raw signed 16-bit values in one blob per map or tileset, with the map's width, height and layers beside it; `anil.harness.world.Table` decodes them and indexes `(x, y, z)` as the game does. One row per tile would be millions of rows read back whole anyway. _(settled 2026-10-01)_
- **Cache tables:** `meta` (the stamp), `maps`, `tilesets`, `events`, `pages` (trigger, `through`, always-on-top, direction fix, walk and step animation, move type, speed and frequency, graphic, and each condition only when it is valid), `transfers` (every code 201 with its first six parameters, and since reader version 10, 2026-10-04, the direction the player must face when an enclosing Conditional Branch tests the player's facing; since reader version 11, 2026-10-04, the branches and choice answers enclosing it as `guards`), `scripts`, `town_map`, `encounters` (step chance per map, version and encounter type), `encounter_slots` (chance, species symbol and levels; both read from GameData objects since reader version 5, 2026-10-02), `switches` (each switch's name from `System.rxdata`, which page selection needs for script switches; reader version 2), `dialogs` (every Show Text, Show Choices and Input Number command by its command index, which warp selection needs; reader version 3, with its text since reader version 4 and its enclosing `guards` since reader version 11), `routes` (every Set Move Route command with its character, its steps' codes and whether it is its page's only command, which arrow tiles need; reader version 11, 2026-10-04), `map_metadata` (each map's `always_bicycle` from the GameData `map_metadata.dat`, which the Surf gate needs, reader version 6, 2026-10-02; and its `outdoor_map` and `town_map_position`, which flying needs, reader version 9, 2026-10-04), `comments` (every code 108 and 408 comment line by its command index, which side stairs need; reader version 7, 2026-10-02), and `calls` (every Call Common Event command, code 117, by its command index with the common event's number, which `train_to_cap`'s candy cups need; reader version 12, 2026-10-07). _(settled 2026-10-01; encounter tables revised and `map_metadata` added 2026-10-02; `comments` added 2026-10-02; `calls` added 2026-10-07; `routes`, every Set Move Route command with the branches enclosing it, which only Pokémon Z's Gogoat climbs read, dropped with them in reader version 8, 2026-10-02, and a narrower `routes` with the `guards` columns added in reader version 11, 2026-10-04, for arrow tiles and lifts)_
- **All script commands, not a recognised subset:** every code 355 and 655 line and every code 111 branch on a script is stored with its command index and indent. Which calls navigation recognises is decided where they are used (bridges, event sizes); storing them all and filtering when the cache is read means adding one never needs a rebuild. _(settled 2026-10-01)_
- **Stamp:** `<reader version>:<build>:<scripts hash>` in `meta`; `open_world` rebuilds when the file is missing, unreadable or carries another stamp. A build writes `.<name>.building` beside the file and renames it into place, so a crash never leaves a half-built cache under the real name. _(settled 2026-10-01)_
- **`anil world`** opens the cache (building it when needed; `--rebuild` forces it) and prints the counts, so the cache can be built and checked on its own. _(settled 2026-10-01)_

### Live Read

- **A separate live-world read, not more `GameState` fields:** `anil.harness.state.read_live_world` returns a `LiveWorld` from two `read` requests, because the per-event paths need the current map's event IDs from the first. Both must answer at the same tick, so callers read as the driver; a mismatch is an error. The whole `$game_switches@data`, `$game_variables@data` and `$game_self_switches@data` are read rather than chosen IDs, since conditions on any map may name any of them. _(settled 2026-10-01)_
- **Current page by `object_id`:** no zero-argument method gives a page's index, so each event's `@page.object_id` is compared with each `@event@pages[n].object_id`, the page count coming from the world cache. No bridge change was needed. _(settled 2026-10-01)_
- **Key items by internal name:** `$bag.quantity("SURFITEM")` takes the name as a string, so no item ID is hard-coded. _(settled 2026-10-01; Añil's names and bag 2026-10-02)_
- **Script switches:** the two kinds that read the event are ported, temp switches (`tsOn?`/`tsOff?`) and cooldowns (`cooledDown?`/`cooledDownDays?`, from `$PokemonGlobal.eventvars` and the game's own `pbGetTimeNow`, which the bridge pins to the launch clock, never the host's `Time.now`). On a map other than the current one temp switches start empty, as on a freshly loaded map, and an autorun or parallel page that sets a temp switch at its top level (`setTempSwitchOn`, outside any branch) is taken to have run when the map loads: its temp switches are set and the page is selected again, until the page selected sets none still off. That is what the game does on arrival: Añil's 53 exit events behind switch 22 (`tsOff?("A")`) run their autorun page once on load and leave their warp page active ([World → Warps](../reference/world.md#warps), "Warps behind a temp switch"). **Decided 2026-10-02:** without it planning from another map selected the autorun page and lost those warps, so map 165 was `unreachable` from map 163. Every other script switch a page condition names is read live, once per plan, as `Game_Event.allocate.switchIsOn?(id)`: the game evaluates it on an event with no state, which is exact because these switches read only global state. A script switch that was not read raises, naming it. **Decided 2026-10-02, forced by evidence:** Añil's page conditions use four such switches (30, 132, 150 and 350; 132, the visible wild Pokémon option, on 273 pages), each arbitrary Ruby over game state, so porting them one by one is not workable and raising would refuse most maps ([World → Event Pages](../reference/world.md#event-pages)). _(settled 2026-10-01; live script switches and load-time temp switches 2026-10-02)_
- **Live events outside the map data get no page:** visible wild Pokémon are `Game_PokeEvent`s in `$game_map.events` with IDs past the map's own and no page in the cache; the live read gives them none, so they block by their graphic and are never warps or touch tiles, and `anil nav check` lists them apart. _(settled 2026-10-02)_

### Passability Port

- **`anil.harness.navigate.Grid`:** one map's tiles, tileset and obstacles plus the player's position, `through`, bridge and vehicle state (Rock Climb's `rockclimb` among them since 2026-10-02); `passable(x, y, d)` follows `Game_Player#passable?` call for call, with the Advanced Items plugin's `Game_Map#passable?` and `playerPassable?`, which replace Essentials' own. A tile-graphic event decides every tile of its footprint, as v21's `at_coordinate?` does. The current map's obstacles are its live events; another map's are its events at their placed tiles with the page selected from the live state. _(settled 2026-10-01; ported to Añil 2026-10-02)_
- **Tile IDs past the tileset:** `Tileset.passage` and `priority` answer `None`, and `terrain_tag` 0, past the table's end, as the game's `Table` answers `nil`, and the port blocks such a tile and skips it for terrain tags and counters as the game does ([World → Passability](../reference/world.md#passability)). _(settled 2026-10-01)_
- **Event order:** the game walks `events.values` in Ruby hash order when deciding a tile by an event's tile graphic; the port goes by event ID, which can differ only when two non-`through` tile-graphic events share a tile. _(settled 2026-10-01)_
- **The check:** `anil nav check` attaches as the driver so the game holds, takes the live read, then one eval returning the game's `passable?` bits for every tile and direction and `counter?` for every tile, and refuses when the ticks differ. Beyond the steps it also compares counter tiles and every live event's page with the port's selection, and exits 1 on any disagreement. _(settled 2026-10-01)_

### Single-Map Walking

- **Costs from the game's own formulas, in `1x` ticks:** a move lasting `t` seconds costs `floor(t / 0.025) + 1` ticks, the updates it takes at `1x`. A step lasts `2 / 2^speed` seconds (0.1 at speed 5, 0.05 at 6), a jump 0.125 s per tile (two for a ledge, one leaving the water), and a slide adds 1 tick before its first tile, then one speed-5 step per tile. The speed comes from the tile the player stands on (ice 5, else diving 3, surfing 4.2, bicycle 6, running 5.5, walking 3.5). A* searches tiles only, without facing, because a turn mid-walk costs nothing. Its estimate is the Manhattan distance times the cheapest step the pace allows. Warp, bump and turn costs are measured on Añil (under [Warps, Cross-Map Routing and Targets](#warps-cross-map-routing-and-targets)), and so are Surf's and Rock Climb's. _(settled 2026-10-01; game time and Añil's speeds 2026-10-02, measured under [World → Movement](../reference/world.md#movement); warp, bump and turn costs measured on Añil 2026-10-02; Rock Climb's 2026-10-02)_
- **A move is a step, a jump or a slide:** a step onto a ledge tile becomes the jump when the landing passes the port's `landable`. A move that ends on ice continues as the slide, and its tiles are the positions the walk expects in order. A slide that would meet a ledge or loop is left out. _(settled 2026-10-01)_
- **A side stair crossing is a move:** a tile covered by an event named `Slope` whose active page is a touch page and whose comments give `Slope: XxY` and `Width:` (with any `Offset:` under a tile, as all of Añil's are) is a stair tile. A step from it toward the stair's x direction, when `passable?` allows that first step and not while surfing, becomes one move to the tile `X` along and `Y` down from it; its tiles are the start row's columns up to the next-to-last, then the end tile, and the last column on the start row is a position the walk may pass without counting. It costs one step per column at the speed of the tile the player stands on, as the game times it. Every crossing is planned from the tile's own event, which is what the game does when the player stepped onto it or reached it by a vertical step within the same stair end; a player standing on one some other way (after a transfer) has no slope, and on two stair ends whose `Width:` rows are numbered inconsistently (map 133 (50, 11) and map 162 (46, 30)) a slope carried by a vertical step can end elsewhere than planned; either strays and replans. Stair tiles are not touch tiles, since the event never runs the interpreter. The A* estimate can overestimate across a stair (a crossing covers `X + |Y|` tiles in `X` steps), which at worst picks a slightly costlier route. _(settled 2026-10-02; [World → Movement](../reference/world.md#movement))_
- **Running:** with the shoes and on foot, the walk holds `ACTION` under runstyle 0 and holds nothing under runstyle 1, which runs unless `ACTION` is held. It stays held through a walk, waits included; while surfing, cycling or diving it is never held, because the vehicle's speed ignores it. `ACTION` has no use on the overworld, so it is held without the checks Pokémon Z needed for C (which started action events and Surf). _(settled 2026-10-01; `ACTION` on Añil 2026-10-02)_
- **Touch tiles:** a tile covered by a current-map event whose active page is player- or event-touch, other than a side stair tile, adds 600 ticks each time a route crosses it. If that page holds a warp, a route never crosses the tile: it ends there only as the target or by taking that warp. A touch page whose transfer is not used as a warp (a dialog on the page, or several destinations other than Ciudad Carmín's pair) is crossed at the 600-tick cost like any touch tile. _(settled 2026-10-01; side stair tiles left out 2026-10-02)_
- **A random wanderer never blocks a planned route:** an event whose active page has move type 1 (random; 334 pages on 97 maps, among them every smoke cloud) is `through` in the grid the search plans on (`Atlas.wandered`), on the current map at its live tile and on any other map at its start tile. It still counts as a touch tile where its page is a touch page. Why: where the page puts it is not where it will be when the walk gets there. Offline, the Torre Pokémon's clouds at their start tiles closed the floor below Mr. Fuji ([Progression](../reference/progression.md), "The Torre Pokémon's smoke clouds"). In a run, three walks in a row ended `unreachable` on the way to Mr. Fuji (map 65 event 2), from the Centro Pokémon, the tower's ground floor and Pueblo Lavanda, though the same walk had gone through from the tower's own floor. With the change, from a snapshot taken there the search found the route through the tower, `anil go --event 65 2` climbed it until a wild battle stopped the walk, and the run went on to reach Mr. Fuji. A step a wanderer still blocks when the walk gets there is marked blocked and replanned, like any blocked step. `anil nav check` compares the game's own passability and leaves wanderers solid. _(2026-10-09, forced by evidence: the three `unreachable` walks to Mr. Fuji)_
- **The walk:** one `advance` holds the move's direction (and `ACTION` when running) until `position` or `signature`, with a 30-tick limit. Input stops when the last step starts, and the walk then advances until the player stands; when the player already stands (at `max` a step ends inside the tick it starts), it advances one tick. When a stop shows a signature not yet seen as the overworld, it is classified with a one-path read (`anil.harness.state.read_context`). On Añil jumps and slides are plain movement that opens no `transition`; the one exception is a side stair's landing, whose `pbWait` reads `transition` ([World → Movement](../reference/world.md#movement)) while the player is on the stair's end tile, and the walk carries on through it as the overworld. Any other `transition` is advanced through with no input, so nothing the walk did not start is pressed; if it returns to the overworld, the walk replans. Any other context, or `event` when a step does not start or the walk ends, ends `interrupted`, unless the player stands on a quiet touch page's tile, whose `event` is advanced through like a `transition` ([Arrow Tiles and Lifts](#arrow-tiles-and-lifts)); anything but the overworld when a replan starts also ends it `interrupted`. A position off the route ends that walk and replans from a fresh live read. _(settled 2026-10-01; the stair landing 2026-10-02; quiet touch pages 2026-10-04)_
- **An event the last step starts:** the interpreter sets up a touched or sighted event only on the tick after the step ends ([World → Warps](../reference/world.md#warps), "When a step-on event runs"). When the player stands at the end of a walk's moves in the overworld, the walk advances one more tick with no input and reads the context again, so an event the last step started ends it `interrupted` rather than `arrived`, except a quiet touch page, which is waited out and the walk replanned ([Arrow Tiles and Lifts](#arrow-tiles-and-lifts)). Session `20261001-211127`: `anil go 3 9 13` from (9, 16) onto event 24's player-touch footprint ended `interrupted` with `event` at tick 2,087, `anil dialog` then read the event's message and ended `finished`, `anil go 3 9 16` back to a plain tile ended `arrived` with the overworld idle, and that one session, holding both walks, replayed with no digest mismatch as `20261001-211207`. A trainer's sight was verified the same way in session `20261001-214417`: a one-step `anil go` into map 14 event 22's sight line ended `interrupted` with the trainer's `message`, once its "!" and walk up passed as `transition` ([Runtime → Context](../reference/runtime.md#context-table)), and `anil dialog` then ended `handoff` on the battle. On Añil the same holds ([World → Warps](../reference/world.md#warps), "When a step-on event runs"): a step onto a warp zone stood on its tile reading `overworld` one tick before the event ran, and in session `20261002-051600` a one-step `anil go` into map 10 event 7's sight line ended `interrupted` with the trainer's `message` once its "!" and walk up passed as `transition`. _(settled 2026-10-01; re-verified on Añil 2026-10-02; the quiet touch page exception 2026-10-04)_
- **Blocked steps:** a step that does not start within 30 ticks waits 20 ticks with no direction and retries, twice. The step (tile and direction) is then marked blocked for this call, and the route is replanned. The walk is `stuck` when no route exists with the marks but one exists without them, or when four replans in a row do not lower the route's tick cost. It is `unreachable` when the live state has no route at all: an NPC that does not wander, standing in the only way, gives this (a random wanderer never blocks the plan, above). _(settled 2026-10-01)_
- **`anil go <map> <x> <y>`** attaches as the driver and prints the first route (moves grouped by direction, with jumps and slides marked and a stair crossing as `stairs <direction> to (x, y)`, the estimated ticks at `1x`, and whether it runs). It then prints the result, its reason, position, steps, warps, flights, replans and the ticks it took at the speed it ran, and the context with its payloads for `interrupted` and `refused`. It exits 1 unless the walk `arrived`. The steps count each planned tile the player reached, including one whose arrival shows on the same stop that ends the walk `interrupted`. _(settled 2026-10-01; the interrupting arrival 2026-10-03, after an egg hatching on the third step of `anil go 28 24 6` counted 2 steps with `$PokemonGlobal.stepcount` at 3, [Runtime → Context Table](../reference/runtime.md#context-table))_

### Warps, Cross-Map Routing and Targets

- **One search over `(map, x, y)`:** A* runs over tiles of every map at once, building each map's grid and exits only when the search first reaches it. The current map uses its live events; any other map uses its placed events with the page selected from the live state, taking the autorun and parallel pages that set temp switches as having run on load ([Live Read](#live-read), "Script switches"; decided 2026-10-02). Before searching, a map-level graph of warps on their active pages gives each map's fewest warps to the target's map. A map missing from it is pruned, and the start missing from it ends the call `unreachable` at once. The estimate is those warps times the warp cost, plus the Manhattan estimate on the target's map. _(settled 2026-10-01)_
- **Which warps:** an event's active page with trigger action, player-touch or event-touch, holding Transfer Player commands that all send to one place, and no dialog command anywhere on the page. A page with a dialog is a conversation, a confirmation or a story scene that navigation cannot check yet, so it is not used, and a target behind it is `unreachable`. [World → Events](../reference/world.md#events) counts the pages this rules out. Autorun and parallel pages are never warps; an autorun that moves the player away whatever the answer is folded into the warps into its map instead ([Arrow Tiles and Lifts](#arrow-tiles-and-lifts)). **Decided 2026-10-02:** the rule first allowed a dialog after the first transfer, and on Añil that took 20 press exits on five events and 20 touch pages that are story scenes (a trainer battle or a cutscene) opening with a Transfer Player within their own map, so a route could start one as if it were a warp. Every page with a single quiet destination and a dialog anywhere sends to its own map, so requiring no dialog at all loses no warp to another map, and no action page is a warp on this build ([World → Warps](../reference/world.md#warps), "Action warps"). **Decided 2026-10-04:** a page whose transfers send to exactly Ciudad Carmín's two maps, 19 and 216, is the warp to map 216 while switch 331 is on and to map 19 while it is off, the same redirect a flight there takes (`MAP_REDIRECTS` and `redirected_transfer` in `anil.harness.world`). Every warp into map 19 has that shape on its first page, so without it the navigator found no route into the town, out of its buildings, or to gym 3 ([Progression → Navigator](../reference/progression.md#navigator)). Other pages with several destinations stay unused as warps; reading a page's Conditional Branches in general was not needed for gym 3. A page that asks a question and transfers from inside its answers is a lift, read under [Arrow Tiles and Lifts](#arrow-tiles-and-lifts) rather than as a warp. _(settled 2026-10-01; no dialog anywhere 2026-10-02; Ciudad Carmín's pair 2026-10-04, forced by gym 3's route)_
- **Exits by the game's trigger rules:** an over-trigger touch warp is a step onto its footprint, taken only as a plain step (no ledge, ice or water-leaving step). Any other touch warp is a bump from a tile next to its footprint whose step toward it is blocked. An action warp is C from a facing tile (next to the footprint or across a counter) after the 1-tick turn, or C while standing on it when it is over-trigger ([World → Warps](../reference/world.md#warps)). A warp edge costs its trigger plus 18 ticks: a step exit pays the 4-tick start from standing (the walk takes every exit from standing) and the step's ticks, a bump 6, and C a 1-tick turn and the C tick. At `1x` that matches the warps measured on Añil, from the first tick of input to the player settled on the destination map: 23 and 24 ticks from a bump, and 30 from a walking step from standing (4 + 8 + 18). A C-started warp is costed the same way but was not measured, as Añil has no plain action warp ([World → Warps](../reference/world.md#warps), "Timings" and "Action warps"). **Decided 2026-10-04:** a page whose every Transfer Player sits inside a Conditional Branch on the player facing one direction (code 111, type 6, character -1) is a warp only by an exit in that direction: a bump, step or C facing any other way runs the page and skips the transfer. The world cache records that direction as `transfers.facing` (`required_facings` and `transfer_facings` in `anil.harness.world`). Seven `FlechaSalida` exit mats have such a page, all needing the player to face down: map 55 events 6 and 24, map 75 events 6 and 19, and map 162 events 8 to 10. Without the rule, `anil go --map 76` along gym 4's route bumped map 75 event 6 from the side at (11, 9) facing left, the event did nothing, and the walk ended `stuck`; with it, `anil go --map 72` from (11, 9) went round to (10, 8), bumped down into the mat and arrived ([World → Warps](../reference/world.md#warps), "Facing branches"). _(settled 2026-10-01; costs measured on Añil 2026-10-02, replacing Pokémon Z's 30-tick warp and 4-tick bump; facing branches 2026-10-04, forced by gym 4's route)_
- **One leg at a time:** the route is split into legs, one per map. The walk takes the first leg's moves, then its exit: a step holds the direction until the position or the signature changes, a bump one tick at a time until either changes or the context leaves `overworld`, and C is one tick. It then advances with no input, reading the context at every stop, through `transition` and `event` (an event the bump saw start counts as seen, so a same-map landing on the destination settles) until the player stands still in the overworld on another map or on the warp's destination. Any other context ends the call `interrupted`. When the event runs but leaves the player on the same map, or nothing starts within 20 ticks, the walk replans. After every exit it replans from a fresh live read on the new map, so the door event's step onto the next tile and the new map's live events are taken as they are. **Decided 2026-10-02, forced by evidence:** a bump held until the position changed kept the direction through a same-map transfer, which has no map load to hold input back, so the player stepped on past the destination with no `transition` or `event` left to see, and the walk waited 20 ticks, replanned and did not count the warp: map 28's stairs (event 8) in `anil go --event 28 22`, 2 warps counted of 3 ([World → Warps](../reference/world.md#warps), "Input held through a same-map warp"). _(settled 2026-10-01; bump release 2026-10-02)_
- **Targets:** a tile target arrives on the tile. A map target arrives on the first tile where the player settles on that map, or at once when already there. An event target arrives on the cheapest facing tile and turns to face the event with one tick of its direction, then checks the position and facing with a live read. An over-trigger action event, which only starts from its own tile, is approached by standing on its footprint instead. The event's position comes from its live state on the current map and from its placement elsewhere, and an event with no active page is `unreachable`. _(settled 2026-10-01)_
- **A touch target steps into its event:** an event target with `touch` (`Target.touch`, which no `anil go` option sets) walks to a facing tile as any event target, then holds the direction toward the event, the bump a warp event takes; a battle or anything else it starts ends the walk `interrupted` there, a `transition` or `event` context at the top of a replan waited out to the context that follows, and an event that ran with nothing started arrives with a reason saying so. An event that has moved on is not there for the step, so the walk replans to where it is now. Why: a player-touch page starts only by a step into it ([Dialog Design → Interact Start](dialog-design.md#interact-start)), a tile target cannot end on a blocking event's tile, and splashes and smoke clouds keep moving. _(build choice, 2026-10-04)_
- **Context table:** the interpreter's own loop frame is `event`, added from a run where an event ran 100 commands in one frame; on Añil it is `038:Interpreter` `block in update` ([Runtime → Context Table](../reference/runtime.md#context-table)). _(settled 2026-10-01; re-keyed for Añil 2026-10-02)_
- **`anil go`** takes `<map> <x> <y>`, `--map <map>` or `--event <map> <event>`. The route is printed by map, naming each exit as kind, direction, event and destination. The result also counts warps. _(settled 2026-10-01)_
- **Names that read one way:** a walk segment that gets where it was going ends `Reached` (it was `Done`, which read like a walk outcome); the walker's helpers are `release_buttons` (not `release`, which names giving back owner control), `take_exit` and `use_exit` (not `take` and `use`), and `wait_until_at` (not `settle`), and `converse`, which answers an exit's prompts, returns nothing when they all went through; `go` returns a `GoResult` (not `Walk`, beside `Driver.walk` and `Walker.walk`). _(settled 2026-10-01)_

### Surf and Climbs

- **The vehicle is part of the search:** A* runs over `(map, x, y, vehicle)`, the vehicle being foot, bicycle or surfing, with one grid and one set of exits per map and vehicle. The start takes the live state; entering the water makes it surfing, and leaving it or a Rock Climb makes it foot. A warp keeps it. _(settled 2026-10-01)_
- **Surf is an exit:** from a tile facing water that can be surfed freely (tags 5, 6 and 7; not a waterfall or its crest), with `passable?` from the tile toward it, the landing passing `passable?(x, y, 0)` while surfing, no event with an active page on the tile, the water tile or across a counter from it, and the water tile not a touch tile. It is only offered with a `SURFITEM` and on a map whose metadata is not `always_bicycle` (maps 110 and 111), read into the world cache as `map_metadata`; badges, the party's moves and companions are not read, as Añil's C-at-the-water path checks none of them ([World → Surf and Climbs](../reference/world.md#surf-and-climbs)). Its one expected dialog is the message naming the trainer, left out when the live read's `$PokemonSystem.animation_item` is 1, and it costs the turn, the C tick, the one-tile jump (6 ticks) and, when the message shows, 18 more: 24 after C, as measured. _(settled 2026-10-01; ported to Añil 2026-10-02: the gate and the dropped confirm are the game's, forced by evidence; reading the bicycle-only maps from `map_metadata.dat` and the animation option live are build choices)_
- **Leaving the water is a move,** a one-tile jump at the surfing speed, as the game makes it from a held step; not onto a ledge or ice. _(settled 2026-10-01)_
- **Rock Climb is an exit:** from any tile inside the map facing a Rock Climb tile (terrain tag 18), the player's own tile a Rock Climb tile or not, on foot, cycling or surfing, with no event with an active page on the tile, the cliff tile or across a counter from it. Its end is the game's own climb run over the terrain tags ([World → Surf and Climbs](../reference/world.md#surf-and-climbs)): straight on while the next tile is a Rock Climb tile, and facing left or right the upper and then the lower diagonal when the straight one is not, then a one-tile jump onto the tile beyond, which with `through` on may be any tile inside the map: a blocked tile (map 116 from (66, 51) down), a ledge, ice or a touch tile. What the game then does follows: on a blocked tile or a ledge nothing, and the walk goes on from there under the ported `passable?`; on ice a slide in the climb's direction with `through` still on, over every ice tile to the first that is not, so the exit ends there and costs the slide as a walked slide does, leaving out the game's `pbWait(0.12)` before it clears the climb flag; a touch tile, crossed or landed on, runs its event and adds 600 ticks each, as on a walk. Three ends are left out, none of which a map has (a script over every map's climb exits on 2026-10-02 found no ledge, ice or warp tile on a climb's path or landing): a slide that meets a ledge (as a walked slide's is) or ends on a Rock Climb tile, which the game would carry on from, and a crossing or landing on a warp tile, which [Touch tiles](#single-map-walking) never lets a route cross. It is only offered with a `ROCKCLIMBITEM`; badges, the party's moves and the vehicle are not read, as Añil's C path checks none of them. Its one expected dialog is the message naming the trainer, left out when `$PokemonSystem.animation_item` is 1, and it costs the turn, the C tick, 10 ticks, 4 per climb step and, when the message shows, 20 more, as measured. It ends on foot. _(settled 2026-10-02, replacing Gogoat climbs: the item gate, the message and the climb's path are the game's; owner 2026-10-02: the earlier build-choice exclusions (a start on a Rock Climb tile or while surfing, and a landing that fails `landable` on foot, is a ledge or ice, or crosses or lands on a touch tile) go, since they can cut off parts of a map, and every case the game's C handler allows is supported without first measuring where each occurs. Built 2026-10-02; the slide and the left-out ends are build choices that follow the walk's own rules)_
- **A Rock Climb settles when the climb flag clears:** the position reads the landing as the jump off the cliff starts, while `$PokemonGlobal.rockclimb` and `through` are still on, so the walk counts the exit done only once a one-path read of `rockclimb` is off with the player on the landing. Since the game drives the climb a tile at a time, waiting for an exit's end tile resets its 20 quiet ticks at every stop that moved the player, still moving or not, for every exit that waits for one (Surf's and Rock Climb's). _(settled 2026-10-02; "still moving or not" 2026-10-02, after a five-step climb at `1x` on map 18 counted 4 quiet ticks at each pause between steps and gave up on the fifth; in the earlier runs each step took 4 ticks, so the pauses counted 3 and stayed under 20)_
- **The dialogs are checked, then answered:** after the C tick the walk reads the context at every stop. A message or choice must match the next expected text (compared word by word, since the interpreter's line ends show as spaces) and options; on a choice the cursor is moved to the expected answer's row (Up and Down, re-reading the live cursor) before C is pressed until the signature changes. Anything else that opens ends the call `interrupted`. When nothing opens within 20 ticks the walk replans. After the last answer it advances until the player stands still on the edge's end tile in the overworld. An exit with no expected dialog skips the check and waits for the end tile at once. Both the expected and the shown text are filled as the dialog layer fills them (markup and control codes removed, `anil.harness.steps.Filler`), since the payload carries the raw text. A step or bump exit with dialogs checks them after its step the same way. The call is finished once the last choice is answered and nothing is open: a text that runs on into the next (ending in `\1`) is pressed through with it, so the texts after the last choice may never show on their own. _(settled 2026-10-01; the no-dialog exit 2026-10-02; the cursor moved to the answer instead of requiring the first option 2026-10-04, for lifts, [Arrow Tiles and Lifts](#arrow-tiles-and-lifts); filled texts, step exits with dialogs and finishing at the last choice 2026-10-04, for the Zona Safari's clerk)_
- **`unreachable` names the edge:** when no route exists, the search runs again as if Surf and Rock Climb were both allowed; if that finds a route using one the live state does not allow, the result names it and its unmet condition. _(settled 2026-10-01)_
- **`anil go`** prints a Surf exit as `surf <direction> onto (x, y)` and a Rock Climb as `climb <direction> to (x, y)`, the landing or, on ice, the end of the slide, and counts neither as a warp. _(settled 2026-10-01; Rock Climb 2026-10-02)_

### Flying

- **Flying is an exit of the search, driven by the menu layer:** the owner put flying with the PokéRider in as the route a pending freeze takes ([Rules Engine Design → The Freeze Route](rules-engine-design.md#the-freeze-route)). From a node on foot or cycling on an outdoor map, with `POKERIDER` and no partner, the search adds an exit to each landing point whose map is visited and whose switch, if any, is on, landing on foot at the point's tile, moved to map 216 for Ciudad Carmín while switch 331 is on ([World → Flying](../reference/world.md#flying)). A leg ending in a flight is taken by `anil.harness.menu`'s `fly` ([Menu Design](menu-design.md)), and the route is replanned from the landing; a flight that does not finish ends the walk `interrupted` (or `stuck` when the game is back on the overworld), naming the menu result. Surfing nodes do not fly: the flight dismounts the bicycle but never clears `surfing` (`pokerider.rb:55`), so it was not tried. _(owner, 2026-10-04; the vehicles and the redirect are build choices from the game's code, `pokerider.rb:14-29`, `:47`)_
- **The flights are added once per map, from every node on the goal map:** the first node the search expands on a map that is not the goal's is that map's cheapest (the estimate is the same on all its tiles), and a flight's cost depends only on the map, so a flight from any later node on it could not be cheaper; on the goal map the estimate varies by tile, so each node adds them. _(build choice, 2026-10-04)_
- **A flight costs 24 ticks plus 2 per cursor square,** the measured cost, the squares counted from the map's own `MapPosition` square (region 0's (0, 0) without one) to the landing point's; a map whose square is in another region than the landing's gets no flight there. The cursor's extra offset on a map that sets `MapSize` and the 64-tick pause-menu delay seen in some calls are left out. The estimate becomes the lower of the warp count's and the cheapest flight's (24 ticks plus the warps from its landing), which keeps it admissible; a start map from which no warp leads to the goal is planned when a flight can. _(build choice, 2026-10-04)_
- **`unreachable` names flying too:** the second search allows it, and the result names "flying with the PokéRider" with `POKERIDER`'s count, or that a partner is following. `anil go` prints a flight as `fly to <town>, map M (x, y)` and counts flights apart from warps. _(build choice, 2026-10-04)_

### Record

- **`note` writes any harness kind but the bridge's own:** it takes a `kind` and an `entry` object and stamps `tick`, `kind` and the client's name, refusing the kinds the bridge writes and an entry that sets those three fields. Replay skips the harness kinds it knows (`nav`, `dialog` from the dialog layer, `menu` from the menu layer, `battle` from the battle layer, `check` from `anil check`, `rules` from the rules guard, and `rules_sleep` from the bridge's rules hooks), so a new harness kind is added to replay's skipped kinds with it. _(settled 2026-10-01)_
- **Three entries, one field naming which:** `stage` is `target` when a call starts (before the refusal check, so a refused call is recorded too), `route` at the first plan and every replan, and `result` when it ends. A route entry holds each leg's map, its exit as `anil go` prints it, and its `path`, every tile its moves pass in order, so progress can be read from the position without further entries. The field was `event` until 2026-10-01, renamed because it collides with game events. _(settled 2026-10-01)_

### Arrow Tiles and Lifts

- **An arrow tile carries the step onto it:** a player-touch or event-touch page that is over-trigger and whose only command is one Set Move Route on the player, its steps all one direction (codes 1 to 4) apart from a sound, the walk and step animation and opacity and blending (codes 31 to 34, 42 to 44), is a push of that many tiles. A step, jump or slide that ends on one goes on along the pushed tiles; a pushed tile that is itself an arrow tile starts its own push from there, as the game triggers it mid-route; a push that meets a blocked step or comes back to an arrow tile it already took is not an edge, since the route would wait on it for ever. Each pushed tile costs a step at the speed the player entered with (the game does not change the player's speed while a route forces it), plus 1 tick per arrow tile triggered. Arrow tiles are not touch-penalised, and a carried tile on a warp is not crossed. The walker needs nothing new: it expects each carried tile in turn, and the route's `event` context was never seen on a walk. Why: maps 83 and 84 of the Guarida Rocket are crossed only by them on gym 5's route ([Progression → Navigator](../reference/progression.md#navigator)); the shape also matches 78 pages on maps 182 and 214, which the same reading covers but which were not walked. _(decided 2026-10-04, forced by gym 5's route)_
- **A lift is a C exit per answer:** an action page's Transfer Player is a lift exit when every branch around it is a `$bag.quantity(:ITEM)>0` Conditional Branch and the innermost is an answer of a Show Choices, and the only dialogs on its way are that choice and the Show Text just before it (the question); since 2026-10-04 any other dialog before the transfer must sit under another answer or a passed bag check's else, as for the clerk below. The exit is pressed like any C exit and answered by moving the cursor to the answer's row and pressing C; it exists only while every item its branches check is held, and a route that needs a missing one comes back `unreachable` naming the item and the event. The answer costs a 20-tick allowance on top of the C exit, an estimate not measured. The world cache keeps each transfer's and dialog's enclosing branches (`guards`: `script <text>`, `else …`, `facing n`, `branch n`, `choice <command> <answer>`, `cancel <command>`), and `choice_transfers` in `anil.harness.world` reads the lifts. Why: Giovanni's office on map 85 is reached only by the Guarida Rocket's lift, which needs `LLAVEROCKET`; the same rule reads the Centro Comercial's lift (map 78 events 12 to 15, four answers, no item), which rode in game too. Autorun pages with a choice and a transfer (maps 103 and 104, the mode menus) are not lifts, as only action pages are; an autorun is read only as below. _(decided 2026-10-04, forced by gym 5's route)_
- **A counter clerk is a step exit per answer:** an event-touch page on an event named `Counter(n)` starts when a step ends on any tile within n tiles in front of the event, by its page's graphic direction ([World → Warps](../reference/world.md#warps), "The Zona Safari's clerk"). When such a page has a choice transfer, those tiles are crossed like a touch warp's (never walked over), and each step onto one from outside them, plain and open, is an exit to the transfer's destination answering its choice. Its dialogs may be wider than a lift's: Show Texts before the question and between the answer and the transfer are expected messages, pressed in turn. Every dialog before the transfer that is not on its path must sit under another answer of a choice on the path or the else of a bag check it passes, so no text the walk cannot predict comes first (`choice_transfer` and `skipped_branch` in `anil.harness.world`). A pressed page keeps the lift rule, question and choice only: the action pages the wider reading would add (map 2 event 35, map 116 event 42 with Bill, map 132 event 16, map 165 event 2) are conversations and story scenes, not doors. Why: the Zona Safari is entered only past its clerk on map 147, whose welcome has three texts before the question and two after "Sí" ([Progression → Gym 5](../reference/progression.md#gym-5)). _(decided 2026-10-04, forced by gym 5's route)_
- **An autorun that moves the player away whatever the answer is part of the warp into its map:** when a map's active pages include an autorun with a choice transfer for every answer, read the same way, every step, bump or press exit into that map from another becomes one exit per answer, ending at that answer's destination with the autorun's dialogs after the exit's own, and the map is never walked. An autorun that lets one answer stay (Koga's gym entrance on map 125, the mode menus, map 2 event 35) is left out, so a walk into its map still stops at its question. Why: on the way out of the Zona Safari, map 147's page 2 on switch 214 asks whether to leave and sends "Sí" to Ciudad Fucsia and "No" back to map 112, so a walk can only cross map 147 by answering it. The page is read from switch 214 as it stands when the route is planned: standing on map 94 with it still on (as the Safari's exit leaves it, at (45, 8) below the strips), a route back in plans the door as the autorun's "No" to map 112, though the strips the walk must cross turn the switch off first; the walk replans after waiting out the strip, and that plan goes through the clerk. This was read from the code, not walked. _(decided 2026-10-04, forced by gym 5's route)_
- **A quiet touch page does not interrupt a walk:** a player-touch or event-touch page with no dialog, transfer, move route or script in the world cache (781 pages on 49 maps, among them Ciudad Fucsia's strips that turn switch 214 off) is waited out: the `event` context it opens while the player stands on its tile is passed like a `transition`, the walk stops pressing until the overworld is back and then replans from where it stands. It is still touch-penalised, and anything else it opens still interrupts. Why: the strips cover the whole row in front of the Safari's door, so the walk from Ciudad Fucsia stopped on them every time. _(decided 2026-10-04, forced by gym 5's route)_

### The Lente de la Verdad

- **Using the lens is an exit to a lens vehicle:** on a map with an event named `#EOT HIDE`, from a node on foot, with `LENSOFTRUTH` held, the search adds an exit from every tile to the same tile with the vehicle `lens`, on foot with the lens in use. In the `lens` grid every `#EOT HIDE` event is `through`; in every other vehicle's grid each is solid, on the current map too, where the live read shows only those within the plugin's range as `through`. Pace is on foot's. A warp out of the map lands on foot. The live read is `lens` while `$scene@eye_of_truth_time` is above 0 and the player is on foot. Why: the plugin makes a hidden event passable only while the lens is in use and the event is within 4 tiles of the player, and a step only ever meets an adjacent one, so while the lens is in use every hidden event the route steps into is open; Koga's gym is crossed only through them ([Progression → Gym 5](../reference/progression.md#gym-5)). ### Story Gates

- **A closed story gate is a blocking edge the navigator owns:** it reads the gates `anil.harness.progression` records, holds a wall's events impassable while the gate's switch or variable is unmet, and a route that needs one comes back `unreachable` naming the gate, with no input sent. Why: reachability stays live in the navigator and the gates are already recorded ([Progression](../reference/progression.md)); a raw walk into a wall comes back `interrupted` on its message. _(owner, 2026-10-04)_
- **A route avoids a story scene's touch strip** (a no-graphic touch page that plays a scene and is not a recorded gate) unless the strip is the walk's own target, so a scene runs only when a goal walks there on purpose. Why: an unplanned scene can start a forced battle mid-walk. _(owner, 2026-10-04)_
- **A closed wall is held in the search, not in the grid:** `anil.harness.progression.GATES` lists every recorded gate; on each map a wall gate whose switch or self switch is off, or whose variable is below its value, closes every tile of its events (their live positions on the current map), and a step, jump, slide, carried push or side stair crossing any of those tiles is not an edge, nor is a Rock Climb that passes one. A gate records no key item, so its flag is the whole test. The tiles stay passable to the grid, so passability and `anil nav check` still match the game's own. A recorded scene stays a touch strip with the touch penalty, so a route that needs it walks over it and the walk comes back `interrupted` on the scene, as the Marowak's spirit did on map 64 strip 22 with switch 98 on. _(build choice, 2026-10-04)_
- **A story scene is a talking touch strip:** a page started by the player's or an event's touch, with no graphic (no character and no tile), that is not a side stair, an arrow tile or a warp, and that has a Show Text or Show Choices, or a script that starts `TrainerBattle` or `WildBattle` (`scene_pages` in `anil.harness.world`). A strip that only moves pictures, like Ruta 10 Sur's strips 13 to 17 on map 24, is not one and is crossed as before. The walk's own target is the goal tiles of a tile target and the strips of an event target, so `--event` toward a scene may cross it on the way to the tile beside it. _(build choice, 2026-10-04)_
- **`unreachable` names the gate or scene from a staged search:** when no route is found, the search is run again with the items held but gates and scenes ignored; a route there names only the closed gates (name, map, events, the flag that opens it and what sets it) and the scenes it crosses. Otherwise the search runs with every ability and gates kept, then with every ability and gates ignored, and names the missing abilities and items as before along with any gate or scene that route crosses. The staging keeps a missing flight out of a gate's message: with every ability at once, the cheapest route out of Ciudad Verde flew within the map before crossing the closed exit. _(build choice, 2026-10-04)_
- **A Rock Climb names the gate or scene it passes:** a climb exit keeps the tiles it passes (`Exit.passed`: the cliff tiles, the landing and any slide), and the staged search's names are read from them as well as from each leg's moves. Why: the search that ignores gates offers a climb across a closed wall's tiles, so before this a route that crossed a closed wall only by climbing came back `unreachable` naming nothing, or naming only Rock Climb when it was not held. Checked offline over the world cache with a test gate on the climb from (11, 6) down to (11, 13) on map 18 and the tiles beside it: switch off, it names the gate; switch on, the route climbs. _(build choice, 2026-10-04)_
