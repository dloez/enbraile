# Battle Layer Design

How the harness acts in a battle: the verbs that answer a command menu with a move and its target, a switch, an item or running, and the one that says where a catch goes when the party is full. Each is driven to the next point that needs the caller and returns a typed result, with no judgment in it. Scoped with the owner on 2026-10-03 and built in four slices, each verified in game before the next (fight and run in singles, switching, items and catching, then doubles, 2v1 and partner battles), followed by the Battle Tower's battles and the full-party catch; this doc owns the layer's design as built. The code is `anil.harness.battle`. It is copied from Wonder's battle layer design (Wonder: `docs/subsystems/harness/explanation/battle-layer-design.md`), where it was built and checked against the game, without Wonder's agent and control panel. Sessions named as evidence ran under Wonder's harness, whose commands, files and bridge module read `wonder` where `anil`'s read `anil` ([Runtime Reference](../reference/runtime.md)). The damage calc that `anil battle` prints with each result is [Damage Calc Design](damage-calc-design.md)'s.

It builds on [Dialog Design](dialog-design.md) (the driver loop, `decision`, transcript, changes, handoff) and [Menu Design](menu-design.md) (the shared steps, `MenuResult`'s shape, and the prompt chains it drives mid-battle). Game facts cited here are owned by [Menus → Battle](../reference/menus.md#battle) (each battle screen and how it was fought), [Runtime → Context](../reference/runtime.md#context) (payloads and the context table) and [State → Battle](../reference/state.md#battle) (the running battle as typed state), where each names how it was verified. The layer needs only Python in WSL and the existing bridge: no Windows-side pieces, no secrets or config, and no models.

## What the Layer Does

Six entry points, each answering one decision (or, for `continue_battle`, driving on to the next) and returning a `BattleResult`:

- `continue_battle(driver)` drives from wherever the battle is (its start after dialog's or navigation's handoff, or after `continue_menu` finishes a chain) to the next point that needs the caller: a command menu (the partner battler's in a double, or the next turn's), a replacement, a prompt chain, or the battle's end. After a battle has ended it ends `finished` with that battle's outcome, wherever the game now is; it is refused only when no battle has run since the game started.
- `fight(driver, move, target=None)` uses a move named by its GameData ID (`fight("THUNDERBOLT", target=3)`), with a target battler (even ours, odd the foe's) when the move's target type leaves a choice. `STRUGGLE` is a move name it takes when the game would use Struggle.
- `run(driver)` runs from the battle, or answers a wild battle's "¿Quieres sacar a otro Pokémon?" with "No".
- `switch(driver, slot)` sends in the party Pokémon in `slot` (`$player.party`'s index), by choice from the command menu, as a replacement (after a faint, U-turn, Baton Pass or an Eject Button), or at the wild battle's next-Pokémon question, answered "Sí" before the pick.
- `item(driver, name, pokemon=None, move=None, target=None)` uses an item named by its ID from the battle bag: on a party slot (`pokemon`), with a move of it for an Ether (`move`), or on a battler (`target`: one of ours for an X item when two stand, a foe for a ball). A Poké Ball's catch is driven through the shakes, the catch message and the Pokédex page to the naming handoff.
- `store(driver, slot=None, bag=None)` answers the `full_party` decision: no `slot` sends the catch to a box ("Enviar a una caja"); a party slot adds the catch to the party ("Agregar al equipo") and sends that member to a box. `bag` answers whether the held item of the Pokémon going to the box goes to the bag.

A verb other than `continue_battle` starts at the decision it answers, named in the `decision` the previous call returned; a partner's battler is the game's AI and never waits on the caller. Calls hold no state: each reads the live game, and calling again continues from wherever the battle is. Real input only, through `Driver.advance`.

## Results

`BattleResult` has `DialogResult`'s shape ([Dialog Design → Results](dialog-design.md#results)), as `MenuResult` does, plus `battle_changes` and `battle_outcome`. `battle_changes` is a diff of the battle between the call's two ends: each battler's species, party index, HP, status, fainted flag and non-zero stat stages by index, who is on the field, the weather, the terrain and the turn, and whether the battle ended. `transcript` holds the battle text, `events` the battle text, `send_out` and `reveal` events, and `changes` our party's changes. All of it is the true state, like every harness result. `BattleResult` also carries the battle at the call's two ends (`battle_before`, `battle_after`), which the knowledge view (`anil.harness.knowledge`) reads to build what a player could see, the damage calc's input ([Damage Calc Design → The Knowledge View](damage-calc-design.md#the-knowledge-view)).

The outcomes are dialog's and the menu layer's:

- `decision`: the game waits on the caller. `BattleDecision` holds the `kind`, the battler and the turn (`@turnCount`):
  - `command`: the command menu, with its prompt and the commands it shows, and the battler's moves with their ID, name and PP. In a double each of our battlers' command menus is its own decision, battler 0's then battler 2's.
  - `replace`: a party screen that cannot be cancelled (after a faint in a trainer battle, U-turn, Baton Pass or an Eject Button), with `slots`, the party slots `pbCanSwitchIn?` accepts.
  - `next_pokemon`: a wild battle's "use the next Pokémon?" after a faint, with `slots`, the question and its options, answered by `switch` or `run`. Its battler is the first of our battlers, in index order, that has fainted and has a Pokémon to send in.
  - `full_party`: a catch with the party full, "¿A dónde quieres enviar a {1}?", with the question and its options, `caught` (the name the question shows), `caught_item`, `slots` (every party slot) and `held` (each party slot's held item), answered by `store`.
- `finished`: the battle is over and the game has reached the first context outside it (not a `transition`), with the battle's outcome (won, lost, fled, caught, draw) from `@decision`. A lost or drawn `canLose` battle, such as Azul's in the lab, first waits a quarter of a second in `after_battle` with the party healed; that wait is a `transition` ([Runtime → Context Table](../reference/runtime.md#context-table)), so the result comes back `finished` in the event that started the battle. What follows the battle (the trainer's lines after it, a whiteout's messages, the rest of the event) is `continue_dialog`'s.
- `handoff`: a prompt chain opened (level-up stat boxes, move learning, evolution, the naming screen after a catch), for `continue_menu`, after which `continue_battle` goes on; or an `unknown` context.
- `refused`: the pre-check found the action illegal, and nothing was pressed.
- `failed`: the game refused a pressed action the pre-check let through and went back to the menu; the reason is the game's message.
- `stuck`: as in dialog and the menu layer.

## Legality and Refusals

Each verb asks the game's own methods whether the action is legal before any input, and ends `refused` when it is not: a move's `pbCanChooseMove?` (0 PP, Disable, Taunt, Encore, Torment, Imprison, the Choice lock), `pbCanSwitch?` and `pbCanSwitchIn?` (trapping), the run checks (a trainer battle, `canRun`, trapping effects, a foe's trapping ability or item), and the bag's chain (`internalBattle`, `nobag`, the trainer-battle item rules, `pbCanUseItemOnPokemon?`, `ItemHandlers.triggerCanUseInBattle`, where the challenge's ball refusal, `disablePokeBalls` and "no effect" live). It also refuses the caller's own mistakes: a move not known, an empty or fainted slot, an item not in the bag or with no battle use, a missing or extra argument, a target not on the field or not allowed by the move's target type. A refusal's reason is the line the game would have shown had the action been tried.

The checks only read: no RNG draw, no state change, so replays and digests stay unchanged. A wild battle's escape roll happens in the run itself, so a run that fails is not a refusal: it spends the turn and the call goes on to the next decision.

## Battle Kinds

Every in-game battle: wild and trainer singles, doubles, 2v1 boss battles, partner battles and the Battle Tower's. In a double, a 2v1 and a partner battle, a turn has a command menu for each of our battlers on the field, one call each; `fight` takes a `target` when the move's target type gives a choice (`battle_target`, [Runtime → Context Table](../reference/runtime.md#context-table)); a partner's battler is played by the game's AI; a 2v1 boss battle's `midbattleScript` messages are battle text, driven like the rest. The Battle Tower's trainers are fought as ordinary trainer battles, under its `nobag` and `setstyle` rules. Each kind was fought to its end with the verbs ([Menus → Battle](../reference/menus.md#battle)).

## Commands

`anil battle continue`, `anil battle fight MOVE [--target N]`, `anil battle run`, `anil battle switch SLOT`, `anil battle item ITEM [--pokemon SLOT] [--move MOVE] [--target N]` and `anil battle store pc|SLOT [--bag|--keep-item]` run the six verbs; move and item IDs are upper-cased. Each attaches as the driver, prints each line as it happens, then the result; it exits 0 on `finished` and `decision`, else 1. At a `command` decision the result is followed by the damage calc for the battle the call ended in, and `anil battle calc` prints it for the running battle without acting ([Damage Calc Design → Battle Annotations](damage-calc-design.md#battle-annotations)). Each takes `--build` before the verb (`anil battle --build NAME fight …`) for the game database the calc reads.

## Observability

Each call writes `battle` entries to `record.jsonl` through `note`, with dialog's `start`, `line`, `step` and `result` stages: `start` names the verb and its arguments, each `step` the screen answered, `result` the outcome with the decision, `battle_outcome`, `battle_changes` and, in a wild battle, `wild_foes`: each foe's species and shiny flag, leaving out a foe already caught, whose battler the game has reset, so a fled or caught shiny shows in the record ([Runtime → Session Record](../reference/runtime.md#session-record)). Replay skips the kind, as it skips `dialog` and `menu`. Steps log to the `harness.battle` logger.

## Decisions

### Scope and Interfaces

- **Done meant every in-game battle kind fought to its end with the verbs:** a wild single, a trainer single, a double, a 2v1 boss battle, a partner battle and a Battle Tower battle, with catches, switches, items and refusals among them, through real input only. _(owner, settled 2026-10-03)_
- **Every in-game battle kind:** wild and trainer singles, doubles, 2v1 boss battles, partner battles and the Battle Tower's; the story needs all but the Tower, and the owner widened it to the game's other kinds of combat. Evidence from the world cache (`world/es-v4.14.sqlite`, the `scripts` table), 2026-10-03: 811 `TrainerBattle.start` and 58 `WildBattle.start` calls besides the overworld's wild battles; 26 `setBattleRule("double")` (one in a gym, map 125) and 27 `setBattleRule("2v1")`, the "Barra Dominantes_Bosses" plugin's boss battles, with `midbattleScript` (79), `cannotRun` (42), `disablePokeBalls` (31), `noexp` and an `outcomevar`; a partner battle, `pbRegisterPartner(:AZULALIADO0, "Azul")` and its two later versions in Bosque Celeste (map 174); `nobag` (11), `canLose` (9) and `setstyle` (14) on trainer battles; and the "Torre Batalla" plugin, which fights its trainers as ordinary trainer battles. _(owner, settled 2026-10-03)_
- **No Safari battles:** a battle kind the game cannot reach is not built. es-v4.14 never starts one: nothing calls `pbSafariState.pbStart`, `pbSafariBattle` or `SafariBattle.new` outside `343_SafariZone.rb` and the debug menu, whose Safari entry only shows a game already running; the world cache's only Safari matches are two Pokémon names and a Safari Ball given as an item (map 78, event 46); no map has `safari_map` set, and `pbSafariState.inProgress?` and `pbInSafari?` read false (session `20261003-201713`). The Zona Safari maps (112–115) hold ordinary wild battles, trainers and a 2v1 raid nest, which the other kinds cover. _(owner, settled 2026-10-03)_
- **Out of scope:** Cable Club link battles (online, against a remote server, and PvP is Showdown's, deferred); judgment of any kind (which move, which switch, when to run or catch), which was Wonder's agent's and does not come over; Mega Evolution and the other battle mechanics the Randomlocke turns off ([Randomlocke Design](randomlocke-design.md)); and going back to the first battler's command from the second's in a double. _(owner, settled 2026-10-03)_
- **The verbs do not enforce the group's honour rules** (a legendary never used, a living shiny in the party): they do what they are told, as the menu layer's do, and the rules engine (`anil.harness.rules`, [Rules Engine Design](rules-engine-design.md)) owns them. The game's own blocks (the "Cláusula Primer Pokémon" ball refusal, `disablePokeBalls`, `nobag`) are refused like any other illegal action. _(owner, settled 2026-10-03)_
- **Battle options keep Añil's defaults:** battle animations, `battle_speed` and `only_speedup_battles` are left alone and the verbs drive whatever they are, to revisit with numbers if battles dominate run time; the max speed preset already makes animations cheap in wall-clock, and the options screen is outside the menu layer. _(owner, settled 2026-10-03)_
- **One command per call:** each verb answers one battler's command menu and drives to the next point that needs the caller. One turn per call was rejected because it needs a list type and still splits at a mid-turn switch; a whole battle per call because it puts a policy, and so judgment, in the harness (Architecture → Cross-Cutting Rules (Wonder: `docs/general/reference/architecture.md` → Cross-Cutting Rules)). It matches the menu layer's one verb per player action, and doubles fall out of it. _(owner, settled 2026-10-03)_
- **A move is named by its GameData ID,** as items are, a target by battler index, a party Pokémon by party slot and a battler for an item by index. Move slot numbers were rejected: they read worse in the record and change meaning after Transform or Mimic. _(owner, settled 2026-10-03)_
- **Results carry a battle diff:** `BattleResult` is `DialogResult`'s shape plus `battle_changes`. The transcript alone was rejected because every caller would diff the state itself; a structured per-action log because it would mean parsing the Spanish battle text the knowledge view rejected as fragile (Knowledge View Design → Decisions (Wonder: `docs/subsystems/harness/explanation/knowledge-view-design.md` → Decisions)). _(owner, settled 2026-10-03)_
- **The outcomes are dialog's and the menu layer's,** with `finished` at the first context outside the battle that is not a `transition`, the only boundary that fits how dialog and the menu layer split contexts. `anil.harness.menu` owns the prompt chains in battle too ([Menu Design → Scope and Interfaces](menu-design.md#scope-and-interfaces)); the battle layer owns the catch's other screens (the shakes, the catch message, the Pokédex page and "sent to the PC"). _(owner, settled 2026-10-03)_
- **The full-party catch is the caller's decision:** like every other choice the game puts to the player in battle, it belongs to the decision maker, and the layer only makes it answerable; an answer that leaves out a choice the game will ask is refused, and nothing is answered by default (`v21.1 Hotfixes/Battle bug fixes.rb:158-249`, the override of `230_Battle_CatchAndStoreMixin.rb` that runs). _(owner, settled 2026-10-03)_
- **Actions are pre-checked with the game's own methods** rather than pressed for the game to refuse. _(owner, settled 2026-10-03)_
- **The pre-check leaks no more than trying would:** a refusal's reason is the message the game would have shown had the action been tried (a trap names the foe's ability, such as Shadow Tag), which a player can always learn for free by trying, since a refused command goes back to the menu without spending the turn. A fact the reason names is recorded in the reveal memory as the game's own message would have recorded it (Knowledge Policy → Story Run (Wonder: `docs/general/explanation/knowledge-policy.md` → Story Run)). _(forced by existing decisions, 2026-10-03)_
- **Four slices, each verified in game before the next,** as the menu layer was built: fight and run in singles, switching, items and catching, then doubles, 2v1 and partner battles; getting doubles working before catching was rejected. _(owner, settled 2026-10-03)_
- **Development verifies from snapshots taken outside a battle** (a snapshot is never saved or restored in one) and from battles started by eval (`WildBattle.start`, `TrainerBattle.start_core`), as the earlier layers did. _(owner, settled 2026-10-03)_

### Build Choices

Taken while building, each verified in game where it rests on the game; the game facts are in [Menus → Battle](../reference/menus.md#battle), [Runtime → Context](../reference/runtime.md#context) and [State → Battle](../reference/state.md#battle).

- **The driver loop is a `anil.harness.steps` `Steps` subclass:** battle messages through dialog's message step, transitions and the rest through its wait step, the command menu as the `command` decision, a sub-menu met by `continue_battle` (fight, target, bag) backed out of with B, the party screen as `replace` and the next-Pokémon question as `next_pokemon`, a prompt chain, a battle choice and an `unknown` context as `handoff`. Outside the battle it waits through transitions and ends `finished` at the first other context. _(build choice, 2026-10-03)_
- **The cursors are read back from the windows:** the command and fight menus' payloads carry a live `cursor`, the scene's `commandWindow` and `fightWindow` `index`, and both grids are walked by Right or Left for the column and Down or Up for the row, read back after each press. A fight menu with fewer than four moves has an empty cell the cursor will not enter, so the column is changed only when the cell beside the cursor holds a move, and otherwise the row first: with three moves, Right from the third (bottom left) toward the empty fourth left the cursor where it was, and in session `20261007-060153` `fight` came back stuck on a Diglett's second move ("the cursor never reached 'Terratemblor' in 6 presses") until the walk went Up first. _(build choice, 2026-10-03; the empty cell 2026-10-07)_
- **The move pre-check is the game's own method with its message captured:** `AnilBridge.move_refusal` runs `pbCanChooseMove?` with the battle's display methods replaced for the call, so the refusal's reason is the game's own line, from a read that draws no RNG and fires no event; `anil replay` matched every digest over a session of them. Before it, `fight` refuses a move the battler does not know. _(build choice, 2026-10-03)_
- **`STRUGGLE` is a move name `fight` takes** when the game would use Struggle (`pbCanShowFightMenu?` false and no Encore), since "Luchar" then picks the move itself; otherwise it is refused. Under Encore the encored move is named as usual and "Luchar" uses it without the fight menu. _(build choice, 2026-10-03)_
- **The run pre-check refuses trapping too, with the game's line:** Sky Drop, a trainer battle (internal or not: running from one that is not forfeits it), `canRun` false, an effect that traps (`trappedInBattle?`), and, after the Ghost type and the escape abilities and items that let the run through, a foe's trapping ability or item, "¡El Rattata salvaje evita que se huya con Sombra Trampa!", each with the game's line. _(build choice, 2026-10-03)_
- **`failed` is the command menu back for the same battler on the same turn** after the verb's input, or the fight menu met again after a move was chosen (backed out of with B); the reason is the last message the call shows, which is the game's refusal. A failed escape roll spends the turn, so it is the next turn's `decision`. _(build choice, 2026-10-03)_
- **The outcome comes from the battle object kept after it ends:** the bridge keeps the last battle it published as `AnilBridge.last_battle`, and the call reads its `@decision` and its final state when the battle it started in has ended, which also gives `battle_changes` their last HP and faint. `battle_changes` compare each battler's species, party index, HP, status, faint and non-zero stages by index, with the turn, weather and terrain, and say when the battle ended. _(build choice, 2026-10-03)_
- **`continue_battle` after a battle has ended ends `finished` with that battle's outcome,** read from `AnilBridge.last_battle`, wherever the game now is, so a caller whose `continue_menu`, or a game running free between command-line calls, carried the battle to its end still gets its `finished` and outcome. Verified in session `20261003-181928` (booted into `reveal-pre`, Chispa set to level 8 and 415 exp by eval against a wild Rattata): `anil battle fight EMBER` ended `handoff` at the stat boxes, `anil menu continue` `finished` at the battle's transition, and `anil battle continue`, attached after the game ran free to the overworld, `finished` (won); `anil replay` (as `20261003-181950`) reported no digest mismatch. `fight` and `run` with no battle running are refused, naming how the last one ended. _(build choice, 2026-10-03)_
- **`switch` finds the row from the battle's party order:** the verb finds the slot's row on the party screen from the battle's party order, moves the read-back cursor there, presses C and chooses "Cambiar". Verified in session `20261003-182739` (booted into `switch-pre`, three Pokémon), whose replay as `20261003-183023` reported no digest mismatch. _(build choice, 2026-10-03)_
- **The switch pre-check is the game's own method with a stand-in party screen:** `AnilBridge.switch_refusal` runs `pbCanSwitch?` at the command menu (with Sky Drop and `canSwitch` checked first, as the command loop and the party screen do) and `pbCanSwitchIn?` for a replacement, handing them an object whose `pbDisplay` keeps the line, so the reason is the line the party screen would have shown; an empty slot is refused before it. _(build choice, 2026-10-03)_
- **A refusal's reveals are recorded by an eval:** after a refusal from the game's checks (`switch`, `run` or `item`), the verb evaluates `AnilBridge.reveal_refusal` on the same check, which passes the line to the reveal hooks' text matching as battle text would, so a trapping foe's ability or item is recorded as the game's message would have recorded it; it is an eval, not a read, so `anil replay` replays it. Against a Rattata given Shadow Tag, `switch` was refused with "¡Sombra Trampa de El Rattata salvaje previene el cambio!" and Wonder's `wonder know reveals`, which does not come over, listed the ability. _(build choice, 2026-10-03)_
- **A pick the game refuses is `failed`:** the party screen met again after "Cambiar" (with the game's line shown in the party screen) ends `failed` with that line, backed out to the command menu when the screen can be cancelled and left open, with the `replace` decision, when it cannot. _(build choice, 2026-10-03)_
- **A chain in battle hands back at the battle's own transitions (forced by evidence):** `continue_menu` waited through every transition after a chain, so after the last foe of a wild battle it ran the EXP bar and the battle's end to the overworld, and the `continue_battle` after it was refused with the outcome unseen (session `20261003-175958`). It now ends `finished` at a transition in battle unless `152:Battle_ExpAndMoveLearning` `pbLearnMove` is in the stack, which holds through the fade into the forget-move screen; with one driver held across the calls, `continue_battle` then ended `finished` (won). From the command line, which lets the game run free between calls, the transition can still run on to the battle's end, and the `continue_battle` after it ends `finished` with the outcome as above ([Menu Design → Build Choices](menu-design.md#build-choices)). _(forced by evidence, 2026-10-03)_
- **`item` finds the row in the battle bag and picks a party slot as `switch` does:** it chooses "Usar"; an item used on a Pokémon (`battle_use` 1, 2) takes a party slot, picked on the bag's party panel by the battle's display order, and an Ether takes a move by its ID. An X item, a Poké Ball and an item with no target take no argument in a single battle. Verified in session `20261003-184811` (booted into `item-pre`), whose replay as `20261003-184859` reported no digest mismatch. _(build choice, 2026-10-03)_
- **The bag rows are the battle bag's own:** the bag opened from a battle lists only items with a battle use, so `AnilBridge.battle_item` reads the item's pocket and row in that filtered list, with its name and use, and the shared `anil.harness.menu.bag_row` walks the read-back pocket and cursor to it, checking the item under the cursor. _(build choice, 2026-10-03)_
- **The item pre-check is the game's chain with its messages captured:** `AnilBridge.item_refusal` runs `Battle#pbItemMenu`'s checks in order: `AnilBridge.bag_refusal` (`internalBattle`, `noBag`, the trainer-battle item rules, which the verb also runs before it looks for the item, below), then `pbCanUseItemOnPokemon?` and `ItemHandlers.triggerCanUseInBattle`, where the challenge's ball refusal, `disablePokeBalls` and "no effect" live. The battle's display methods and the top-level `pbMessage`, `pbSEStop`, `pbSEPlay` and `pbPlayBuzzerSE` are replaced for the call, since the challenge's refusal is a top-level `pbMessage`. Before it, after `bag_refusal`, the verb refuses an item that is not in the bag or has no battle use, a missing or extra party slot or move, and an empty slot. A refusal is followed by the `reveal_refusal` eval, as for `switch` and `run`. _(build choice, 2026-10-03)_
- **"Mochila"'s own refusals come before the bag checks (forced by evidence):** in a Battle Tower battle (`nobag`) with no Potion in the bag, `item` was refused "Poción is not in the bag", where the game says "No se pueden usar objetos en este combate." on "Mochila" whatever the item (session `20261003-202022`). The checks that need no item, `internalBattle`, `noBag` and the trainer-battle item rules, are now `AnilBridge.bag_refusal`, which the verb runs before it looks for the item and `item_refusal` runs first; a Tower battle started the same way then gave the `noBag` line (session `20261003-202915`). _(forced by evidence, 2026-10-03)_
- **A Poké Ball in a trainer battle is refused (forced by evidence):** the ball passes every check the bag makes. It is then thrown, spent and blocked with "¡El Entrenador bloqueó tu Ball! ¡Robar está mal!", and the turn is lost (session `20261003-184506`). So the pre-check refuses it with that line, except for a Snag Ball at a Shadow Pokémon, which the game lets through. This widens the game's own blocks to an action the game takes but that can never succeed. _(forced by evidence, 2026-10-03)_
- **The catch's screens are driven by the battle loop:** the throw, the shakes and the catch jingle are `transition`s. The Pokédex page of a newly owned species is the `pokedex_entry` context, closed with C and noted as a `step`, and the forced name screen is the `naming` chain's `handoff`. After `continue_menu` names it, `continue_battle` shows "Se agregó a Rattata al equipo." and ends `finished` (caught). The bag's party panel or commands met again after the action are the game's refusal, backed out of to the command menu as `failed`. _(build choice, 2026-10-03)_
- **`fight` and `item` work in every battle size:** each of our battlers' command menus is its own `decision`, battler 0's then battler 2's. `fight` in a battle that is not one against one reads the move's target data and the battlers the target screen names (`AnilBridge.move_targets`, from the scene's own `pbCreateTargetTexts`); a move with one target takes `target`, a battler the screen names and that is on the field, and the verb walks the screen's read-back cursor to it; with only one such battler it takes no `target` and the verb picks it; a move with any other target type takes none and the screen is confirmed with C. A target the move cannot take, or one not on the field, is refused with the battlers it can take. In a single battle `fight` refuses any `target`. Verified in session `20261003-192618`, whose replay as `20261003-193255` reported no digest mismatch ([Menus → Battle](../reference/menus.md#battle)). _(build choice, 2026-10-03)_
- **Encore's target is the caller's too:** in a double "Luchar" under Encore shows its line and opens the target screen for the encored move, so the verb drives the line and aims as for a chosen move. _(build choice, 2026-10-03)_
- **`item`'s `target` is a battler index for an X item and a ball:** an X item with two of our battlers on the field (`pbPlayerBattlerCount` 2) needs `target`, one of ours on the field, and the verb picks that battler's party slot on the bag's party panel; with one of ours (a single battle, a partner battle) it goes to the battler choosing and any other `target` is refused. A ball's `target`, when given, must be a foe on the field, since the game itself refuses a ball while two stand and picks the one left.
