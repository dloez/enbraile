# The DJ Bot

The group's music bot for the `TemploManchego` Discord server (`757377019536211988`), in `dj/bot/`. Members use slash commands or the [panel](../../panel/explanation/panel.md) to play music in the voice channel they are in.

## How It Runs

One Node process (`src/main.ts`) runs in the `dj` namespace of the owner's home Kubernetes cluster, `tom` (two Raspberry Pi CM5 boards, arm64), declared in the owner's `dloez` repository under `homelab/apps/tom/dj.yaml`. It holds:

- the Discord gateway connection (`src/client.ts`). Slash commands arrive over it (`src/discord.ts`), so the application has no interactions endpoint URL.
- voice, with `@discordjs/voice`, `@snazzah/davey`, yt-dlp and ffmpeg (`src/player.ts`).
- the queue, settings, playlists and history (`src/session.ts`), in SQLite through `node:sqlite` (`src/db.ts`), on a 1 Gi Longhorn volume at `/data`.
- an HTTP API for the panel behind `INTERNAL_SECRET`, plus the `/live` WebSocket (`src/api.ts`). The socket opens with a ticket the HTTP API signs for one user; it pushes each new view and answers the same calls as the HTTP API, as that user, so the panel need not go through Vercel while it is open. Every 10 s the bot pings each socket, closing one whose last ping went unanswered, and sends a `beat` message the panel listens for.

A `tailscale` pod beside it publishes the API with Tailscale Funnel at `https://dj.<tailnet>.ts.net`, over an outbound connection, so no port opens on the home network.

A song is resolved by yt-dlp before it plays, and that is nearly all the wait before audio. Audio counts as started only when the first packet plays: until then the view says `loading` and its position stays at the start. Once audio plays, the next three songs and the one before are resolved ahead, so a skip, the next song or going back starts in about 300 ms; a resolved song is kept for 2 hours, well inside the roughly 6 hours its YouTube audio link lasts; resolving them alongside the song that is still loading would slow that one on the Pi. A song whose audio stream stops more than 5 s before its end did not finish: YouTube refused or dropped the stream, and ffmpeg ends its output the same way as at a real end, which used to skip the song silently. The bot resolves it again, this time with yt-dlp's cache off, and resumes from where it stopped, once; if that is cut short too, the song has failed. Audio that stops arriving without the stream ending is a stall: a watchdog checks every 250 ms whether the played audio moved, and after 1 s without it, or while the voice connection is down, the view says `stalled` and its position freezes until audio flows again; the re-resolve after a cut stream counts as stalled too. The audio player waits up to 10 s of missing audio before giving up on a stream, so ffmpeg's own reconnects can bridge a network hiccup; the library's default of five 20 ms frames turned every 100 ms hiccup into a cut stream and a re-resolve of several seconds. A song that fails to play is skipped, and the view's `skipped` names it until the next song plays, so the panel can say so. Each view carries the live position from the player, not the one the tick last saved. The panel can also warm a YouTube watch URL it expects the user to pick; at most two warms resolve at once, and more are dropped. Every yt-dlp run, a resolve, a warm, a check that an autoplay pick plays or a playlist expansion, waits for one of two slots, and background work (preparing ahead, warms, checks) may hold only one of them, so the song about to play always has a slot; a song that must start now jumps the queue, even when the same song was already waiting to be prepared. yt-dlp is the onedir release build, which starts without unpacking itself, and runs with `--plugin-dirs yt-dlp-plugins`, whose one plugin (`ejs-player-cache`) turns on yt-dlp's cache of the preprocessed YouTube player, so the JS challenge of each song after the first is solved without parsing the player again. The cache lives in the pod's `~/.cache/yt-dlp`, about 4 MB per player version, and is lost on each restart.

A tick every 15 seconds while a session runs saves the position, rejoins and resumes if the voice connection dropped, and applies the leave rules. A dropped voice connection does not wait for the tick: one that has not started reconnecting by itself within 5 s is joined again at once, and the song resumes from where its audio stopped, not from the position the last tick saved, up to 15 s earlier. A song paused at the time stays paused through a rejoin, a restart of the bot or a volume change: the bot keeps the position and loads the audio only when someone resumes. Before, each of these started the song playing again. When someone drags the bot to another channel, it follows: the session takes the new channel, so the listener count and the leave and autoplay rules watch the channel it is in. On shutdown the process saves the position, and on start it rejoins and resumes a session that was running. A rejoin that fails, on start or after a drop, is tried again on the next ticks, and only after four failures in a row does the session end; before, one failed join on start ended the session and lost its queue.

## Deploying

A push to `main` touching `dj/bot/` runs `.github/workflows/dj-bot.yml` on GitHub's arm64 runner. It runs the gate and pushes `ghcr.io/dloez/enbraile-dj-bot:main-<UTC timestamp>-<short sha>`. Flux in `tom` scans the repository every minute, picks the newest tag (`clusters/tom/dj-image-automation.yaml` in `dloez`), commits it to `dloez`'s `main`, and rolls the pod. See [How to Run and Deploy the Bot](../how-to/run-and-deploy-the-bot.md).

## Decisions

- **Three autoplay picks lined up, never redone once shown:** the view carries `autoplayUpcoming`, up to three picks that always follow the queue, so the panel shows what autoplay will play even while songs are queued. A shown pick stays: adding a song, clearing the queue, someone joining or leaving the call or switching the mode only changes the picks made for slots that open later, when a pick plays, is swapped or will not resolve. Only turning autoplay off and on starts a new set. A pick is checked to resolve once it is within three songs of playing; one that will not is dropped, replaced in its place, and not offered again until the bot restarts, so a shown pick that cannot play no longer turns into a surprise skip. Supersedes swapping the whole set when a song was added (2026-11-07). _(owner, 2026-11-07; the mechanism the agent's choice)_
- **Swapping a pick:** the `swapPick` command marks one lined-up pick `replacing` and keeps it in its slot until a new pick arrives and takes that same slot; if the radio finds none, the slot is dropped. The swapped song is not picked again until the bot restarts. Panel only. _(owner, 2026-11-07; kept in its slot while replaced at the owner's request, 2026-11-08; the mechanism the agent's choice)_
- **At most two yt-dlp runs at once, one of them for background work:** each run is a Python process with a Node child solving YouTube's challenge, and panel actions started several at once (a swap or an added song prepares three songs ahead and one behind and checks the autoplay picks, beside the panel's warms), which got the pod killed for memory mid-song. Background preparing now runs one song at a time, a few seconds each, well inside a song's length. The pod's memory limit lives in `dloez`'s `homelab/apps/tom/dj.yaml` and was left at 768 Mi. _(owner reported music stopping on panel actions, 2026-11-08; the numbers the agent's choice)_
- **Each song keeps who asked for it:** a queued song carries `requestedBy` (the Discord user ID, or `autoplay`) and `requester`, the member's server display name and avatar, read through the guild members REST endpoint when the song is added, so it needs no privileged intent; history saves both (`requester_name`, `requester_avatar`), and rows from before have none. An avatar link can go stale when someone changes their avatar, and the panel then shows their initial. _(owner asked to show the author in the queue and history, 2026-11-07; the mechanism the agent's choice)_
- **Autoplay modes, Session and Explore:** `autoplayMode` is kept across sessions, `session` by default, set from the panel only. Session is the session radio below. Explore blends one radio from the taste of the people in the voice call: for each listener, up to three songs they asked for themselves (never autoplay's), drawn at random weighted by how often they asked for each, each listener's seeds summing to the same weight, at most 12 seeds in all, so a large call takes fewer per person. The seed songs themselves are not picked. A listener with no history adds nothing, and if nobody in the call has any, Explore picks as Session does; each pick records which mode chose it (`pickedBy`), and every Explore plan logs `explore seeds` with the listeners, how many had history and the seed count. The panel's suggestions stay on the session radio in both modes. _(owner, 2026-11-07; the numbers the agent's choice)_
- **A session is the bot joining a voice channel until it leaves:** the bot opens a row in `listening_session` when it joins with nothing running and closes it in `end()`; a move to another channel, a rejoin or a restart of the bot stays in the same session. Each history row carries its `session_id` and, for autoplay picks, the mode that chose it (`picked_by`). History from before sessions existed was grouped on first start, a new session after a gap of more than 30 minutes. Repeat-queue replays the session, since the session's track list is the queue. The API lists sessions newest first with their song and autoplay counts, who asked for songs and up to four covers (`/internal/sessions`), lists a session's songs in play order, each once (`/internal/session/tracks`), saves a session as a new playlist (`/internal/playlist/from-session`) and merges one into a playlist of the user's, skipping songs it already holds (`/internal/playlist/merge-session`). Queueing a session again and saving or merging it take every song that played, autoplay picks included. _(owner, 2026-11-08; the 30-minute split of old history and the API the agent's choice)_
- **History keeps 20,000 rows, not 200:** Explore reads each listener's asks across past sessions, and 200 rows covered a few days of the group's listening. A row is a few hundred bytes, so the cap is a few MB on the 1 Gi volume. _(2026-11-07, the agent's choice)_
- **A stall freezes the clock instead of letting it run:** the panel counted on from the last view while no audio played, for the seconds a cut stream took to re-resolve or until the next tick's position pulled it back, so the bar moved over silence. The 1 s threshold, the 10 s missing-audio limit and the 10 s beat are the agent's numbers. _(owner reported the bar moving over silence, 2026-11-08; the mechanism the agent's choice)_
- **A song is ended with five frames of silence before the next starts:** a skip, previous, seek or any other switch stops the old song the library's normal way, which sends five Opus silence frames (100 ms), and the new song's audio starts only after them; a paused or not yet playing song is stopped at once, since nothing of it is in flight. Stopping without them, as `player.stop(true)` did from 2026-11-07, left listeners' Opus decoders on the old song's state, so a fragment of it played after the gap, as Discord's voice docs warn. The cost is 100 ms more before a new song. _(owner reported the old song playing for a moment after a skip, 2026-11-08; supersedes the forced stop of 2026-11-07)_
- **Three songs ahead and one behind, kept 2 hours:** two ahead missed a double skip, and the song before had often expired from the 30-minute cache by the time someone went back. Each extra resolve costs a few seconds of the Pi's CPU, run only after the current song plays. _(owner, 2026-11-07; the numbers the agent's choice)_
- **Previous:** the `previous` command restarts the song after 3 s of play and before that goes back one in the track list, which keeps the songs already played. Only the panel sends it; there is no slash command. _(owner asked for a previous button, 2026-11-07; the 3 s rule the agent's choice)_
- **On the owner's cluster `tom`, not Cloudflare:** the bot is one long-lived process holding gateway and voice connections, which Workers, a Durable Object and a Container that woke on demand only imitated, slowly and for about $5–8 a month. Supersedes running on Cloudflare Workers Paid with Containers (2026-11-06) and deploying through Workers Builds (2026-11-07). _(owner, 2026-11-07)_
- **API published through Tailscale Funnel, not a Cloudflare Tunnel:** both are outbound-only, so no port forwarding and the home IP stays private, but Funnel's public relays are Tailscale's own servers rather than Cloudflare's IPs, which Spanish ISPs block during LaLiga matches, so the panel's live socket should keep working then. No report of a LaLiga block hitting Tailscale was found on 2026-11-07; if one does, the panel polls through Vercel as before. The cost is a `ts.net` address instead of an `enbraile.vip` one, which only the panel sees. _(owner, 2026-11-07)_
- **Deployed by Flux image automation pushing straight to `dloez`'s `main`,** unlike pihole's reviewed bumps, so a push to `enbraile` deploys like the panel does. _(owner asked for deploy on push, 2026-11-07)_
- **Either node:** the pod has no node pinning. _(owner, 2026-11-07)_
- **No monthly hour limit:** it only capped the Container bill. _(owner, 2026-11-07)_
- **`@discordjs/voice` with yt-dlp and ffmpeg, not Lavalink:** a single TypeScript process rather than a Java server in a TypeScript repo. _(2026-11-06)_
- **History keeps each song's artist, cover and length,** so the panel's "Played before" shows them; `src/db.ts` adds the columns to an older database on start, and rows from before have none. _(2026-11-07)_
- **SQLite on the pod's volume:** one guild and one process, and nothing else reads it. _(2026-11-07)_
- **One shared Discord application,** renamed `Enbraile`, for login and bot; private, server installs only, with View Channels, Connect and Speak. _(owner, 2026-11-06)_
- **Access:** members of one DJ role, picked on the panel by a user with Manage Server; until one is picked, only Manage Server users. No votes to skip. _(owner, 2026-11-07)_ A member's roles are fetched from Discord again when the bot last read them more than 30 s ago, since the bot has no Server Members intent and so hears of no role change; before, a role given later counted only after the bot restarted. The panel's "You need the DJ role" screen asks again every 30 s and opens once the role counts, so a new DJ need not reload. _(owner asked how a new DJ gets in, 2026-11-08; the 30 s the agent's choice)_
- **Sources:** YouTube Music song search, YouTube videos and playlists, and Spotify links, matched to YouTube by title from Spotify's embed page so no credentials are needed. _(owner, 2026-11-07)_
- **YouTube Music songs, not YouTube videos, for text:** plain YouTube search returned music videos, covers and other versions. `src/music.ts` asks YouTube Music's web API for songs only, which needs no key. The panel lists its results; `/play` with text takes the top song. Spotify tracks resolve lazily, as a `music.youtube.com/search?…#songs` URL that yt-dlp plays the first song of. If that API changes, `/play` falls back to the lazy search URL, and the panel reports that search failed and asks for a link. _(owner asked for better results, 2026-11-07; the mechanism the agent's choice)_
- **yt-dlp's onedir build, default player clients:** the single-file build unpacks itself on every run, and no other player client returned playable audio. A faster JS runtime (deno, bun) was not measured. _(2026-11-07)_
- **The preprocessed player cache, turned on by a plugin:** upstream leaves it off because the files are large and never rotated; a pod's cache is wiped at every deploy, which bounds both. If a yt-dlp release renames `EJSBaseJCP`, yt-dlp logs the plugin's import error and plays on without it (checked 2026-11-07); if it renames only the flag, the plugin silently does nothing, which the `resolved … ms` log lines would show. _(owner asked for faster starts, 2026-11-07; the mechanism the agent's choice)_
- **Leaving:** 1 minute after the channel empties, 5 after the queue ends, 15 while paused. _(owner, 2026-11-07)_
- **Autoplay picks from a session radio:** `radioPicks()` in `src/session.ts` fetches YouTube Music's radio (`RDAMVM<videoId>`, the `next` endpoint, no key) for the last five songs people asked for, newest weighted most, plus the last two autoplay picks at a fraction of that. Each song in each radio scores the seed's weight times the square of how near the top it sits, so a song several seeds agree on wins. It skips songs under 1:30, which YouTube Music's radios mix in as interludes (6–9 of 50 in one on 2026-11-07), anything in the queue or the last 100 played, and anyone among the last three songs' artists unless nothing else is left. The pick arrives with its cover, artist and length. The panel's suggestions are the same ranking, one song per artist, kept for ten minutes per set of seeds, and seeded by queued songs too. It replaces the YouTube video mix of the last song alone, which the owner found kept to the same artist. YouTube Music has no radio from several songs, hence the blend. _(owner asked for a Spotify-like radio from what was played, 2026-11-07; the mechanism the agent's choice)_
- **Autoplay:** off by default, stopping after an hour without human action and as soon as nobody is in the call. Keeping it going is a button on the panel; the bot posts nothing to Discord, since that would need Send Messages. The next autoplay song is picked when the last queued song starts, preloaded, and shown at the end of the panel's queue. _(owner's rule, 2026-11-07; the panel-only prompt chosen by the agent when told not to ask, so open to change)_
